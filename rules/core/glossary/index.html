An alphabetical reference to the core rules, character options, equipment, adversaries and optional modules. Entries explain terminology rather than catalogue individual abilities. Module-specific entries identify their module. Full procedures and explicit exceptions remain in the referenced rules.

A

Advance (character advancement). A permanent improvement purchased with 6 XP. Options include skills, pools, Edge, Refresh, Effort Level, tags, Specialisations and abilities. See also Advancement; XP. Core Rules §12.2. Advance (Effect). Spend 1 Power per segment on an established progress clock. A procedure may also permit removing progress through sabotage. It cannot substitute a duplicate clock for Disposition or bypass a disputed objective without an established relationship. Core Rules §10.6 Module E.10. Advancement. Character development through XP and Advances. Meeting an improvement’s eligibility threshold makes it available; it does not grant it automatically. Core Rules §12. Adversary. Opposition represented by a simplified entry with action ratings, Grit, Guard, tags and listed features. Ordinary adversaries have no character pools or generic Effort. Woe funds only listed purchases; it grants no generic purchase of additional Power. See also Grit; Rank; Role; Special. Adversaries §1 Core Rules §2.6. Affliction. A harmful condition with an ongoing or unusual cause, such as disease, poison, possession or a curse. Its source determines worsening, treatment and recovery rather than the ordinary injury-healing intervals. Core Rules §6.6. Anchor skill. A playbook’s defining skill, which must occupy a +3 or +4 position in the starting skill pyramid. Core Rules §3.3. Area effect. An effect reaching everyone within its established area unless specified otherwise. Ordinary conflict loss applies once to shared Disposition with its additional-participant bonus; explicit statuses resolve separately against affected targets. Core Rules §10.17. Armour. Protection against imposed physical harm. PC armour adds tier reduction when the wearer Resists; ordinary adversaries use their own reduction procedure. It does not normally reduce Disposition loss or protected payments. Generic Playbooks §2.3. Artifact. In Module A, a reusable special item with tags, usually an ability, and a depletion rating. Powerful artifacts require attunement. See also Attunement; Depletion rating. Module A.2. Asset. A concrete resource, person, position or capability supporting an action. Permission, Tag Power, a Situational Condition, Help and Superiority each require their own justification; an asset does not automatically grant them all. Core Rules §10.3 Module E.6. Asset tag. In the faction modules, a tag describing a resource the faction controls or can access. Each relevant asset supplies +1 Power, maximum +2, rather than a roll modifier. Module E.2. Attack. Outside scripted conflicts, an Effect inflicting a harmful status at 1 Power per tier. As a scripted action, it instead removes opposing Disposition equal to committed Power +1, requiring at least 1 committed Power. It inflicts no status without explicit permission. Core Rules §5 §10.6. Attunement. In Module A, the bond required by an artifact marked ★. Each attuned artifact costs 1 Refresh while attuned; a PC may attune to two at most. Module A.2. Automatic success. Replaces an otherwise permitted action’s roll, not its permissions, stakes or scripted interaction. Use its specified Power plus applicable Tag Power unless already included; add no margin or critical bonus. Grants no extra turn or rescue of a trumped action. Independent consequences still apply. Core Rules §2.5 §10.5.

B

Backing. Supporting a side at conflict setup through a meaningful contribution and accepting its stakes. Each eligible backer adds +1 starting Disposition. Backing costs no pool points and is not Help. Core Rules §10.2. Belief. A conviction paired with a commitment to act. Each PC has a World, Other and Self Belief. Beliefs guide decisions and can justify compels, but supply no modifier or Power. Core Rules §3.5. Bodies. Members represented by a minion-group entry. Outside conflicts, Attacks can remove Bodies under the minion rules; ordinary conflict loss does not. The group is one participant, not one per Body. Adversaries §6.6. Bonus die. An additional action d12. Always retain the designated Complication die and highest single other action die. At most two bonus dice from all sources, including free dice. Resistance dice and the separate Risk die use their own procedures. Core Rules §2.4. Boosted skill. In Module F, a marked skill that restores 1 point to the paying pool after Buying an Insight. Recovery is after payment, not a discount, and does not prevent running dry. Module F.4. Burned tag. A relevant tag made temporarily unusable to gain +1 Power on a success, outside the usual +2 Tag Power cap. Recover it through the applicable downtime or milestone procedure. Core Rules §3.4. Buy. In Module F, gain one Insight without rolling by paying 4 − relevant pool Edge, minimum 1. Pay in full. See also Insight; Probe. Module F.2.

C

Campaign Truth. A foundational setting or campaign fact, normally agreed at session zero. Changing one requires a major arc, not a scene-scale Create purchase. See also Scale; Truth. Core Rules §8.2. Capstone (◆). A playbook’s final ability. Available after 12 Advances and at least four abilities from that playbook; costs an Advance but no Refresh. A character may have only one. Core Rules §12.2. Case clock. In Module F, the six-segment They Know We Know clock tracking opposition response to noise or delay, including a Probe complication that costs time or makes noise. Completion prompts decisive action, not disappearance of required clues. Module F.5. Check. In Modules B and D, permission for a Players” Turn activity or downtime endeavour. Earned through the specified tag- or Scar-based expedition penalty, or granted by the module’s free allowance. A Check permits an attempt, not automatic success. Modules B.4 and D.2. Clean Success. A successful action with the Complication die lower than the other retained die. Generates normal success Power and no comparison-based complication. Independent triggers still apply. Core Rules §2.5. Clock. See Progress clock. Clock stage. A named intermediate step in a long project, distinct from a character-development milestone. Module D.5. Cohesion. A faction Quality measuring loyalty, legitimacy, discipline and shared identity. Permanent Cohesion 0 creates a Fracture crisis; effective Cohesion reduced to 0 by Strain does not. Modules E.2 and E.12. Compel. An offer of meaningful trouble arising from a tag, Scar, Belief or Instinct. Accept for 1 Tychē Point, or refuse by paying 1. It changes the situation, not an automatic numerical penalty or a resolved success. An accepted compel’s complication has no severity and can’t be bought off, mitigated or Resisted; the GM gains no Woe for it. Core Rules §4. Complication. Trouble arising from an established danger when a rule triggers it. State its concrete consequence and minor, moderate or major severity before choosing a response. The GM may add one Woe-bought complication per roll after it resolves and before the player responds, paying 1/2/3 Woe for minor/moderate/major. Original-severity Woe buyoff precedes Power mitigation and eligible Resistance. Complications cannot cancel or empty purchased Effects; scripted-conflict limits still apply. Core Rules §2.6 §10.7. Complication die. The designated action d12 that must always be retained. Compare its final face with the highest other action die retained alongside it to classify the outcome. Separate from the optional Risk die. Core Rules §2.1 §2.5. Compromise. A cost or limitation attached to victory, determined by the winner’s final Disposition relative to its original adjusted start. Minor, Significant and Major describe negotiated outcomes, not automatic injuries. Core Rules §10.13. Concession. A negotiated end proposed before the next roll. State the opponent’s gains, retained interests and every accepted cost. Accepted terms replace further resolution unless they say otherwise. Core Rules §10.12. Condition. See Situational Condition. Grind hardships use their own rules, not ordinary rated Conditions. Module B.3. Conflict. A sustained struggle whose stakes, active opposition and meaningful tactical choices justify scripting. Each side has an intent, shared Disposition and a three-action script. See also Exchange; Script; Volley. Core Rules §10.1. Conflict action tag. An equipment tag supplying Tag Power to a named action, such as +1 Attack. It is not a roll modifier and cannot duplicate the same item’s general tag benefit. Generic Playbooks §2.2. Conflict type. The form of struggle, such as Fight, Chase, Argument or Negotiation, determining setup and action skills. Core Rules §10.4. Contact Truth. A relationship with a named person and what it makes possible. It grants no automatic Standing, reputation tag, free Help or extra turn. Core Rules §3.6 Module D.7. Core clue. In Module F, information needed to reach another scene or understand the case. A fitting skill at +1 or higher finds it without cost or roll; missing expertise must not make it permanently inaccessible. Module F.1. Create. Spend 2 Power for a justified scene Truth, or 2 per Situational Condition rating. +1 Power allows it to outlast the scene, subject to its ending condition. Core Rules §5. Critical. An action outcome from matching final retained dice: Critical Success on success, Critical Failure on failure. Double 1s and double 12s have no special action overrides. Exact versus ties receive no comparison-based critical. Resistance uses a separate procedure. Core Rules §2.5 §6. Critical Failure. A failed action with matching final retained dice. Generates no Power and triggers one major complication within the named danger and stakes. Cancelling or reducing the complication does not turn failure into success. Core Rules §2.5 §2.6. Critical Success. A successful action with matching final retained dice. Adds +1 Power to normal success Power and a fitting flourish, not an unpaid Effect. Independent complications still apply. Core Rules §2.5. Cypher. In Module A, a one-use item or effect of tier 1–6. Usually no roll: its tier supplies one Power budget for its listed Effects. Fixed written benefits instead happen as stated. Module A.1. Cypher Limit. In Module A, the number a PC can safely carry, normally two. Extras generate Woe and risk a Volatile result at scene end. Module A.1.

D

Danger. An explicitly established hazard or listed harmful outcome, not something inferred from a high tier or rating. Record exposure, consequence and avoidance or mitigation. Core Rules §8.5. Danger clock. A clock tracking an approaching threat, advanced by its trigger, deadline or permitted Woe spend. Ordinary failed conflict actions do not automatically tick it. Core Rules §7. Declare. Spend 1 Tychē Point for a minor scene fact. It can supply permission or an asset, but cannot resolve an action, buy numerical statuses or Conditions, or rewrite larger-scale Truths. Core Rules §4. Defeat. A side loses at 0 Disposition or when nobody can pursue its objective. Defeat is not automatic injury or death. See also Compromise; Mutual defeat; Taken Out. Core Rules §10.12. Defend. A scripted action restoring 1 shared Disposition per Power committed, up to the original adjusted start. It does not normally restore pools, heal statuses, repair gear or return Taken Out participants. Core Rules §10.6. Definitive care. The procedure, facility, specialist or explicit healing effect needed to lower a life-threatening tier-6 injury. Stabilisation alone stops the deadline, not the injury. Core Rules §6.7. Depletion (recovery category). Exhaustion, strain or temporary overload recovered through depletion rests. Restoring its pool and clearing its status are separate uses of recovery. Core Rules §6.5. Depletion rating (artifact). In Module A, the chance of an artifact becoming unusable after use: roll d12, and deplete it on the rating or lower. Module A.2. Descriptor. The adjective in the character sentence: +2 points to one pool, two fixed double-sided tags and a suggested Instinct. Core Rules §3.1. Disarm. A 3-Power Maneuver purchase disabling a named weapon, asset or key tag for the conflict. It removes an action category only if that asset was the side’s sole plausible means of performing it. Core Rules §10.6. Discover. Spend 1 Power per true, useful GM answer. In conflicts, questions must follow from the successful action, not unrelated or remote investigation. Core Rules §10.6. Disposition. One shared total measuring a side’s ability to pursue its conflict objective, not individual health. Losing it does not automatically drain pools, inflict statuses or remove participants. Core Rules §10.6. Disrupt. Spend 3 Power to cancel or delay an established prepared action, or remove 1 available GM Woe. It cannot generically cancel, reveal, replace or delay committed scripted actions. Core Rules §10.6. Doubles. Matching final retained action-die faces, including matches created by explicit face changes. Use the normal critical procedure; matches do not override opposing totals or exact ties. Resistance’s multiple-12 procedure is separate. Core Rules §2.5 §6. Double-sided tag. One description that can support actions and cause meaningful trouble, not separate strength and weakness tags. See also Compel; Tag. Core Rules §3.4. Downtime. In Module D, safe time between expeditions for recovery, preparation, projects and relationships. Checks buy endeavours; arrival is not a general resource reset. Module D.1. Dying. Life-threatening tier-6 physical harm under lethal stakes starts a three-segment death clock. Stabilisation stops it; definitive care addresses the injury. Core Rules §6.7.

E

Edge. A pool rating reducing Effort costs and adding Resistance dice. It adds nothing directly to ordinary skill or recovery rolls. Core Rules §3.2 §6.5. Effect. A result purchased with Power. Purchased Effects determine what happens; success does not automatically grant an unpurchased goal. Core Rules §5. Effort. Pay 3 − relevant pool Edge, minimum 0, for one bonus die before rolling or +1 Power after success, including eligible automatic success. Normal Effort limits apply. Lost, tied and trumped actions do not qualify for post-success purchases. Core Rules §2.4. Effort Level. Maximum Effort uses on one roll. Starts at 1; advancement can unlock 2 and 3. Core Rules §2.4 §12.2. Endeavour. In Module D, focused downtime activity purchased with a Check: Recuperate, Prepare, Make and Mend, Investigate, Develop, Connect or Sustain. Module D.4. Ending condition. The duration or event ending an effect. An early-removal condition does not extend its stated duration. See also Scope. Core Rules §5. Enhance. Spend 1 Power per tier for a helpful personal status. Normally available on Defend or Maneuver in conflicts. Core Rules §5 §10.6. Entanglements. Module C’s collaborative session-zero story map connecting PCs to people, organizations, objects, places and pressures. Relationships establish fiction, not automatic bonuses. Module C.7. Escalation. A special item or action tag generating Woe on its listed trigger. It supplies no Power and does not count toward the tag cap. Core Rules §9.3. Exchange. Three volleys of a scripted conflict. If it continues, both sides commit a new exchange. Core Rules §10.5. Extra Feat. Spend 1 Power for a related secondary accomplishment within the action’s scope. No extra turn or roll, duplicate primary Disposition effect, or disguised prohibited purchase. Core Rules §10.6.

F

Faction. An organized group pursuing a shared purpose. Module E uses Qualities, assets, reach, projects and relationships rather than PC pools. Module E.1. Failure. A final total below Difficulty, or a losing versus total. Generates no Power, including Tag Power, and no automatic failure Woe. Final retained faces determine Failure with a Benefit, Failure with Complication or Critical Failure. Exact ties and trumped actions receive none of these outcomes. Core Rules §2.5 §10.5. Failure with a Benefit. A failed action with the Complication die lower than the other retained die. Generates no Power. The GM names a useful fictional opening, not accomplishment of the failed goal or a free mechanical benefit. Core Rules §2.5. Failure with Complication. A failed action with the Complication die higher than the other retained die. Generates no Power and triggers a complication whose severity follows its fictional impact. Cancelling or reducing it does not accomplish the failed goal. Core Rules §2.5. Favour. In Module E, an acknowledged promise recording debtor, creditor, service, limits, notice, timing and settlement. It is not unlimited debt or control over the debtor. See also Obligation. Module E.7. Feint. A misdirection action using Attack’s Disposition-loss calculation. Independent against Defend, trumped by Attack, versus Feint or Maneuver. Core Rules §10.6. Floating clue. In Module F, a clue placed at an appropriate source the group investigates, rather than fixed to one easily missed location. Module F.6. Focus. The character sentence’s verb phrase: two fixed tags, a PC connection and tiered abilities describing a distinctive pursuit or capability. Generic Focuses §1.1. Focus tier. One of three ability levels. Tier 1 is free at creation; Tier 2 is eligible after three Advances and Tier 3 after six. Higher-tier abilities require purchase. Generic Focuses §1.1. Fracture. Module E’s four-segment crisis clock at permanent Cohesion 0. Completion transforms or breaks the organization; people and assets do not vanish. Module E.12. Free. Waives only the specified cost, not timing, permission, action requirements, caps, risk or other limits. Generic Playbooks §1.2.

G

Gain Position. A 1-Power Maneuver purchase granting one bonus die to the side’s next scripted action. Only one may be pending per side. A no-roll action consumes it unused. Core Rules §10.6. GM's Turn. Module B’s expedition phase, when obstacles and action rolls advance the Grind; not a scripted conflict turn. Module B.1. Goal. A concrete current objective with an observable completion point: what the character wants next, rather than why they act. Core Rules §3.5. Grind. Module B’s accumulating pressure of time, exertion and hostile circumstances. Its repeating clock imposes hardships as it fills. Module B.2. Grind hardship. Hungry & Thirsty, Weary, Exposed, Shaken, Sick and Broken, gained in that order under Module B. Each has specific effects and removal requirements, no ordinary tier, no stacking and no Resistance. Module B.3. Grit. An adversary’s contribution to starting shared Disposition, not an individual health track to deplete. Adversaries §2. Guard. Added to Standard 15 when a PC’s independent conflict roll targets that adversary. It modifies Difficulty, not a versus total. Adversaries §2.

H

Haven. In Module D, shelter, food and sufficient safety to begin downtime. Arrival does not automatically refill pools, heal injuries or remove Scars. Module D.1. Healing interval. Qualifying recovery time removing one lasting-injury tier after appropriate care and removal of its cause. Length depends on current tier. Core Rules §6.6. Help. A relevant ally pays 1 pool point to add one bonus die and shares the consequences. Free Help waives only that cost. Helping is not a scripted turn. Core Rules §2.4 §10.5.

I

Identity tag. One of six starting tags from Descriptor, Playbook and Focus. Equipment and abilities may supply additional tags under their own rules. Core Rules §3.4. Impede. A 1-Power Maneuver purchase imposing −3 on the opposing next scripted action roll. Not a status; does not stack, and a no-roll action consumes it unused. Core Rules §10.6. Independent test. A test against Difficulty, not an opposing total. Independent conflict actions normally use Standard 15 with explicit adjustments; both finish before victory is checked. Core Rules §10.5. Injury. A lasting harmful status representing damage rather than temporary depletion. Recovery uses appropriate treatment and healing time unless an explicit healing exception applies. Core Rules §6.6. Injury trade. Once per character per conflict, accept a tier-2/3/4 lasting injury to cancel 1/2/3 incoming ordinary Attack or Feint loss. Only one participant may trade per action. Core Rules §10.11. Inquiry. Module F’s investigation scene, separating guaranteed core clues from optional Insight. Module F.1. Insight. Module F’s information or benefit beyond a core clue, gained through Probe or Buy: detail, leverage, contact, spotlight or foresight. Module F.3. Instinct. A recognizable trigger and habitual response. Acting takes no time, but does not guarantee success or bypass required rolls or conflict limits. Core Rules §3.5. Intent. What a side gains by winning. Victory does not grant unrelated objectives or consequences beyond the agreed stakes. Core Rules §10.2. Interval (downtime). One full week in Module D. Injury-healing intervals run alongside it using their own rules. Module D.2. Invoke. Spend 1 Tychē Point, justified by a tag or Truth, to reroll and replace the current roll or add +2 after rolling but before resolution. An action reroll leaves the separate Risk die unchanged unless explicitly permitted; Invoke is permitted and rerolls it, and the new result replaces the old. The +2 affects only the total, sits outside status and Condition slots, and is not Tag Power. Core Rules §4.

J–K

Journey clock. Module B’s journey-completion clock. Travel roles advance it or address associated needs; progress is separate from conflict Disposition. Module B.5. Kept dice. Also called the retained dice. The designated Complication die and highest single other action die. Retention is mandatory; do not select a lower die to create a match. Use this pair for both total and outcome comparison. Explicit face changes preserve die identity and do not reopen discarded dice. Core Rules §2.1 §2.8. Kind. An adversary category such as Human, Beast or Guardian supplying traits and available Specials; distinct from Rank and Role. Adversaries §4. Knowledge Skill. An unrated expertise establishing knowledge and permission, not dice or modifiers. Routine professional facts need no roll. Core Rules §3.3.

L–M

Lead-in. In Module F, a clue pointing toward another scene. Multiple lead-ins provide alternative investigative routes. Module F.6. Lethal stakes. Death made possible before the roll or exchange that could cause it, not automatic death on defeat. Core Rules §10.2. Load. Equipment capacity chosen before the mission: Light 3, Normal 5 or Heavy 7. Declare playbook-kit items as needed. Generic Playbooks §2.1. Major ability (★). An ability costing 1 extra Refresh to acquire, separate from its activation cost. Generic Playbooks §1.2. Major milestone. The end of a full campaign arc, distinct from an ordinary milestone. Can permit changing Focus with GM approval. Core Rules §12 Generic Focuses §1.1. Maneuver. A scripted action establishing advantages through purchases such as Impede, Gain Position, Disarm, Create and permitted supplementary Effects. Core Rules §10.6. Mētis. Speed, precision, stealth and reflexes; commonly Resists traps, falls, capture and restraint. Core Rules §3.2. Milestone. The end of a story arc or completion of a major Goal, called by the GM. Permits specified character revisions; not a project clock stage. Core Rules §12. Modifier. A numerical roll adjustment. Personal statuses and Situational Conditions have separate helpful and harmful slots; explicit fixed modifiers follow their rules. Tags and Truths supply no roll modifier themselves. Core Rules §2.1. Mutual defeat. Both conflict sides are defeated. Resolve compatible parts of both intents and a shared setback within stakes, not an outright winner or winner’s compromise. Core Rules §10.12.

N–P

Obligation. A favour viewed from its debtor’s side, owed within recorded terms. It does not remove player control or create an unlimited promise. Module E.7. Other Belief. A conviction and commitment concerning another PC, built on an agreed relationship premise. It grants no authority over that character. Core Rules §3.5. Overrun. Final pool cost minus remaining points, minimum 0. Only Resistance and imposed costs normally overrun; excess determines incoming running-dry tier. Core Rules §6.1. Participant. A character or group able to contribute to a side’s objective and bound by its stakes. Participation grants no separate Disposition track. Core Rules §10.2. Permission. The expertise, tools, credentials, Truth or other fictional basis making an approach possible. Not a bonus or guaranteed success. Core Rules §2.2. Personal exception. In Module E, a relationship changing one character’s faction treatment. Its relationship Condition replaces rather than stacks with the party’s for that subject. Module E.4. Personal status. A physical, mental or emotional state of tier 1–6. Apply the highest relevant helpful and harmful tiers; its scope may also restrict possibility. See also Stacking; Tier. Core Rules §5.1. Phased boss. An eligible adversary designated before setup to use Boss Phases. Thresholds use a fixed starting-Disposition reference. Rank or importance alone grants no phases. Adversaries §9. Playbook. The character sentence’s noun, defining role and methods through base pools, Edge, an anchor skill, tags, abilities, XP trigger, gear and relationship prompts. The six generic playbooks are Adept, Bellator, Explorator, Furcifer, Interlocutor and Savant. Generic Playbooks §1.1. Players' Turn. Module B’s post-mission phase for spending Checks. Module D replaces it with its fuller downtime procedure when enabled. Modules B.4 and D.1. Pneuma. Will, presence, faith, emotional resilience and the supernatural; commonly Resists fear, despair, possession, corruption and social pressure. Core Rules §3.2. Pool. One of Sōma, Mētis, Psychē and Pneuma: expendable reserves for Effort, Resistance and specified abilities, not attributes added to skill rolls. Core Rules §3.2. Power. An action’s budget from outcome, relevant tags and explicit sources. Buys permitted Effects; unspent Power normally becomes Weal. Distinct from roll total and Disposition loss. Core Rules §2.5. Power mitigation. Spend eligible surplus action Power to reduce a complication one severity band per Power: major → moderate → minor → none. Reserve required Effect Power first. Woe buyoff closes once mitigation begins; eligible Resistance follows it. Scripted actions have additional reservation and timing requirements. Core Rules §2.6 §10.7. Prepared in Advance. An optional once-per-session Tychē spend for plausible earlier preparation. It cannot contradict events, undo consequences or supply a special flashback ability’s broader permissions. Core Rules §4.2. Probe. In Module F, a skill test investigating beyond a core clue. Each Power spent on Discover buys one Insight; failure does not remove the clue. Module F.2. Progress clock. A segmented track of work or approaching developments. Advance normally buys one segment per Power; each procedure defines triggers and completion. Core Rules §7. Project. Work too large for one action or endeavour, tracked by a clock. Completion grants its established result, not unrelated benefits. Modules D.5 and E.10. Protected payment. A consequence accepted for a benefit, including injury trades, running-dry statuses and agreed concession costs. General Resistance, prevention, reduction, immunity and redirection cannot negate it. Later treatment follows its own rules. Core Rules §10.7. Psychē. Intellect, perception, memory and cunning; commonly Resists confusion, illusion, deception and disturbing knowledge. Core Rules §3.2.

Q–R

Quality (faction). One of Might, Treasure, Influence, Territory and Cohesion, rated 0 to +4 and justified by actual resources. Not added directly to PC rolls. Module E.2. Rank. Adversary danger category: Minion group, Minor, Established, Great or Paramount. Sets construction values, distinct from Kind and Role. Adversaries §3. Rating. A Situational Condition’s value, or another specifically named feature’s value. A Condition rating is not a status tier and does not itself cause a Scar or Take Out. Core Rules §5.2. Recovery roll. Rest-generated points distributed among pools or permitted depletion recovery. Edge does not apply; recovery does not automatically heal injuries, erase Scars or restore Disposition. Core Rules §6.5. Red herring. In Module F, true but misleading information with a source and reason, not a narrator’s false statement presented as fact. Module F.6. Refresh. The session-start Tychē baseline, normally 3. Abilities and attuned artifacts can reduce it; purchases cannot lower it below 1. Core Rules §4.1. Relationship Condition. Module E’s Situational Condition for recognized faction dealings where relationship affects access, cooperation or credibility. Uses the normal Condition slot, not a separate bonus. Module E.3. Resistance; Resist. Reduce an incoming harmful status by 2 tiers or an imposed narrative complication by one severity band, including minor to none—not both from the same Resistance. Normally roll 2 + Edge d12, read the highest and pay its pool cost; the roll determines cost, not whether reduction occurs. Each distinct complication may be Resisted once, after any Power mitigation. Negative Edge has its own procedure. Does not normally affect Disposition loss or protected payments. Core Rules §6 §2.6. Restore. Spend 1 Power per tier to reduce a harmful status. Lasting injuries retain treatment limits unless an explicit healing exception applies. Core Rules §5 §6.6. Risky roll. Optional Risky 1–3 uses a separate Risk d12. At or below the rating, it triggers an independent complication on any outcome. Never retain it, add it to the total or count it toward the bonus-dice cap. Generic action rerolls and face changes leave it unchanged unless explicitly permitted; Invoke rerolls it, and the new result replaces the old. Core Rules §2.7. Role. An adversary’s tactical specialty, such as Brute or Bulwark, identifying its best action and a role trait. Adversaries §3. Rotation. Continuous conflict participation order. Each available participant acts once per round; no consecutive volleys unless only one is available. Helping is not a turn. Core Rules §10.5. Rumour. In Module C, an unconfirmed claim. Investigation establishes what is true; this procedure cannot overturn already established facts. Module C.4. Running dry. A positive cost empties or overruns a pool. Finish the triggering action or Resistance, then apply incoming running-dry tier 1 + overrun. An empty pool is not itself an ongoing penalty. Core Rules §6.1.

S

Scale. A Truth’s reach: Scene, Site, Region or Campaign. Not strength or a modifier; a scene purchase cannot rewrite a larger-scale fact. Core Rules §8.2. Scar. A lasting mark from a harmful breaking point or explicit trigger. No Power or roll modifier, but can be compelled. Normally at most one per scene; the fourth retires the PC from ordinary adventuring. Core Rules §6.3. Scope. Who or what an effect reaches and when it applies. Limits modifiers and fictional restrictions; a broad name alone does not establish broad effects. Core Rules §5. Script. Three secretly committed actions and assigned actors, revealed one volley at a time. Paid changes normally retain the actor. Core Rules §10.5. Self Belief. A personal principle or identity conviction with a commitment showing how the character intends to live by it under pressure. Core Rules §3.5. Shared endeavour. In Module D, collective work costing two pooled Checks. One lead rolls, others may Help, and participants share the result. Module D.4. Shrug. An adversary’s limited free Resist reducing imposed status harm by two tiers. Extra Shrugs cost Woe; ordinary NPCs cannot combine repeated reductions on one application. Adversaries §2. Stacking. Repeated applications of the same status or Condition use six boxes. Mark the incoming value’s box, or the next empty box to its right if occupied; retain earlier marks. Stakes. Established possible consequences of winning or losing, including capture, death, exposure or permanent injury. They constrain outcomes and cannot widen retroactively. Core Rules §10.2. Standing. Module E’s faction policy toward the party, from −3 Enemy to +3 Allied. Not membership, authority, affection, fear or Cohesion. Module E.3.
