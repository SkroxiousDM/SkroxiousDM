Combining Modules

Choose the modules the campaign needs. Use an explicit replacement instead of running both versions of the same procedure:

  • Downtime (D) replaces Module B.4’s Players” Turn procedure. Core field rests and Module B’s Grind-condition recovery remain separate (D.1).

  • Factions (E) replaces Module C.5’s basic faction action procedure. Its starting-cluster procedure E.1A replaces C.8; retain the rest of Module C.

  • Downtime with Factions uses one World Turn per stay, after endeavours resolve (D.7, E.10). A

longer stay does not create extra World Turns or faster Strain recovery.

  • Inquiry (F) adds clue and Insight procedures to Inquiry scenes. It doesn’t replace conflicts, and relabelling a scene doesn’t reset one.

Module rules override core rules only where they say so: the Grind’s 2-Woe tick isn’t the danger-clock rate, and a cypher’s no-roll use isn’t an extra scripted turn. Pools, statuses, Disposition, project clocks and faction Qualities stay separate unless a rule links them.

Anything a module doesn’t cover is an open ruling, not permission to add effects, costs or resets. Each module lists its own.

Module A — Cyphers & Artifacts (Cypher)

Cyphers are frequent, disposable power-ups that encourage players to use them rather than hoard them. Artifacts are rare, reusable items that may run out.

  • What they are: one-use items or effects — potions, grenades, scrolls, nanite injectors, blessed relics, strange tech. Each has a tier (1–6).

  • Using one: an action, usually with no roll. The cypher generates Power equal to its tier, spent only on its listed Effects. Some create a Truth instead. Fixed benefits, such as restore 3 Sōma, happen as written and aren’t Power.

  • Cypher Limit: each PC can carry 2 cyphers. A setting ability may raise this.

  • Over the limit: at the end of each scene, the GM gains 1 Woe per extra cypher and may trigger a Volatile result (roll d12: 1–4 one cypher fires randomly; 5–8 one cypher is ruined; 9–12 nothing happens yet).

  • Subtle cyphers (setting option): instead of physical objects, cyphers are luck, insights, or favours ("a contact owes you", "a flash of insight"). They work the same way and fit low-magic or modern games.

  • GM pacing: give out 1–2 cyphers per PC per session. They should feel like loot that gets spent.

One Power budget. Split the Power among the listed Effects; each Effect doesn’t get the full budget. Create costs 2 Power per rank (Core Rules §5): a tier-2 Smoke charge buys rank-1 smoke cover, and a tier-4 Firebomb that buys a rank-2 burning Truth has nothing left for its Attack.

In a scripted conflict, using a cypher still takes a scripted action, or a trigger the item states. "No roll" means automatic success on an action that’s otherwise allowed: it doesn’t rescue a trumped action, change the script or bypass rotation, and it can’t be used while Taken Out (Core Rules §10.5). With no dice, there’s no critical or complication.

Which action. A cypher with Attack among its Effects is used on a scripted Attack or Feint; any other cypher is used on a Maneuver. The Ward token is the exception: use it when a status would be inflicted on you, with no action, once per scene. Harm and protection. An ordinary Attack removes Disposition, not a status. A status-inflicting cypher inflicts its listed status and removes no Disposition unless it says so. The Ward token can’t negate Disposition loss, running-dry statuses or other protected payments (Core Rules §10.7).

Healing. Restore on a cypher follows the normal limit for lasting injuries: treatment removes at most 1 tier, unless the item says otherwise (Core Rules §6.6). Restoring Sōma or Pneuma doesn’t clear statuses, refund injury trades, erase Scars or return a Taken Out ally to the conflict (Core Rules §6.4).

Area. Words like grenade or blast don’t create an area effect by themselves. An item with an explicit area effect uses Core Rules §10.17.

Cypher format:

Flashbang (Tier 3) — Effects: Attack (dazed), Weaken. Form: palm-sized canister. Draught of Iron Skin (Tier 2) — Effects: Enhance (armoured), Restore. Lasts one scene.

A complete entry also states its action or trigger, targets, duration, removal conditions and any area or conflict exceptions.

A.2 Artifacts

  • What they are: reusable items of real power — a flaming sword, a psychic amplifier, a starship's AI core.

  • Tags: an artifact has 1–2 power tags (counting toward the +2 cap) and usually a special ability. Its item tag and conflict gear tag don’t both count for the same benefit on one roll (Core Rules §10.10).

  • Depletion: each artifact has a depletion rating, e.g. 1 in d12. After each use, roll d12; on that number or lower, it is depleted (broken, drained, or needs a downtime clock to restore).

  • Attunement: powerful artifacts (marked ★) must be attuned and cost 1 Refresh while attuned (Core Rules §4.1). A PC can attune to a maximum of 2 artifacts.

  • Artifact quirks: each artifact has a weakness tag that can be compelled for Tychē Points (thirsts for blood, talks too much, draws the Hunters).

Artifact abilities follow the same conflict limits as cyphers. Switching equipment doesn’t refresh a once-per-conflict benefit or restore a depleted item, and Defend restores Disposition only.

Artifact format:

★ Ember-Brand (longsword) — Tags: blade of living flame, +1 Attack (conflict gear). Ability: spend 1 Power to add burning to any status you inflict. Depletion: 1 in d12 (goes cold until bathed in a forge). Quirk: cannot be hidden.

Ember-Brand only adds burning to a status something else inflicts. Its Attack tag doesn’t inflict a status, and Disposition loss doesn’t become burning.

Module B — The Grind & Travel (Mouse Guard)

The Grind turns time, distance and hardship into slow, steady pressure. Play alternates between a GM’s Turn (the mission) and a Players” Turn (downtime).

B.1 Session Structure

Phase

What happens

GM's Turn

The mission. The GM presents obstacles, and rolls advance the Grind (B.2).

Players' Turn

Downtime after the mission. Players spend Checks to take actions: recover, research, craft, pursue Goals.

B.2 The Grind Clock

  • During the GM's Turn, the GM keeps a Grind clock visible to the table.

  • Each action roll ticks it by 1; Resistance and recovery rolls don’t. In a scripted conflict, count 1 tick per exchange, not per volley, actor or die.

  • Size by hostility: 4 (harsh: desert, deep winter, the underworld), 6 (normal wilderness or dangerous city), 8 (gentle or well-supplied).

  • When it fills: every PC gains the next Grind condition they don't have yet, then the clock resets. The GM may also spend 2 Woe to tick it.

The Grind uses its own rates, not the generic danger-clock rate, and failed conflict actions don’t add ticks. It doesn’t remove Disposition or cause injuries.

When it fills in a conflict, apply the condition at the end of the current exchange, or when the conflict ends if that comes first. Outside a conflict, apply it at once. The timing never cancels an action that resolves on its own (Core Rules §10.5).

Grind conditions count as statuses for Restore, but they don't stack and have no tier: the Order column is the sequence you gain them in. Broken already includes its Scar; don’t add a tier-6 Scar too.

Separate layers. A condition that stops a PC contributing removes them from the conflict without costing Disposition; the side loses when nobody can pursue its objective (Core Rules §10.9). Clearing a condition doesn’t refill pools, heal injuries, erase Scars or let a PC rejoin a conflict (Core Rules §6.4–6.6).

No Resistance. Grind conditions are hardship, not harm: they can’t be Resisted, only cleared. Ordinary tiered harm still uses core Resistance.

B.4 Checks (Players' Turn)

  • Earn Checks during the GM's Turn by applying one of your weakness tags or Scars to your own roll (−1). You earn 1 Check, or 2 if the penalty makes the roll fail.

  • Everyone gets 1 free Check in the Players' Turn.

  • Spend 1 Check for one Players' Turn action: a recovery roll against a Grind condition or status, a downtime clock roll, a research or crafting roll, restoring a burned tag, attuning an artifact, or a scene that pursues your Goal.

  • Sharing: you can give a Check to another PC whose scene you take part in.

Weaknesses become a resource: hurt yourself now to recover later. A Check permits an activity; it doesn’t guarantee success. Lasting injuries still follow Core Rules §6.6, and another Check doesn’t clear the treated mark. Module D, if used, replaces B.4.

B.5 Travel

A journey is a Journey clock (6 for a short trip, 8 for a hard trip, 12 for an expedition). Each leg (a day or a stage), the party assigns roles; each role makes one roll:

  • Supplies: the party tracks Supplies as a counter, not a status (start at 3–6). Lose 1 per leg. At 0, everyone becomes Hungry & Thirsty automatically and the Grind clock shrinks by 2, to a minimum of 3.

  • Terrain and weather are Truths that set Difficulty (Muskeg, Whiteout, Rapids, Washed-out Road).

  • Encounters: the GM spends Woe (1 for a minor encounter, 3 for a major one), or uses the Scout's failed roll.

  • Portages and hard stretches: a special leg (Endure or Athletics roll for every PC); failure ticks the Grind clock twice.

Journeys and conflicts. Advance buys 1 Journey segment per Power (Core Rules §7). Journey progress and Disposition don’t affect each other. A travel encounter uses the conflict test (Core Rules §10.1): one obstacle may need a single roll, while a sustained chase may be a conflict. Travel roles don’t become conflict actions.

Spot Trouble cancels a prepared encounter, not a resolved action or an ongoing conflict. Moving from travel into a chase doesn’t reset a conflict already under way (Core Rules §10.2).

Prices. Each role option costs 1 Power; Make Time advances the Journey clock 1 segment per Power. A failed hard stretch ticks the Grind clock twice in total, replacing the normal roll tick.

C.4 Rumours

A rumour is an unconfirmed claim, not yet an established Truth. When players test it through Discover, investigation or arriving, the GM rolls d12 to determine what is actually true:

d12

1–4

5–8

Use the table only for an unsettled rumour, never to overturn something already established. Record what’s actually true, including how it differs from the claim. Discovering a hidden Truth reveals it; it doesn’t create the fact you wanted.

C.5 Faction Turns

With Module E, use its World Turn (E.10) instead of this section, after the stay’s endeavours if Module D is in use (D.7). The rest of Module C still applies.

Without Module E, between sessions (or each in-world week), the GM takes a World Turn:

  1. Each active faction rolls 2d12 + its rating (+1 to +4) vs. Standard 15 for its current project.

  2. Power ticks the faction's project clock (sized by the change it makes, below).

  3. When a clock fills, the faction creates, removes, or changes the appropriate Site or Region Truth (e.g. The Guild controls the river replaces The river is free). Campaign Truths still require a major arc, not an ordinary completed clock.

  4. PCs who hear about it can respond; PC actions can tick faction clocks forward or back using Advance.

A faction project changes a Site Truth with an 8-segment clock and a Region Truth with a 12-segment clock. A Campaign Truth changes only through a major arc (C.6).

C.6 Player Influence on Truths

  • Discover effects and Momentum spends reveal hidden Truths.

  • Create effects and 2 Momentum create Scene Truths; bigger changes need a progress clock (8 segments for a Site Truth, 12 for a Region Truth).

  • Major arcs can rewrite Campaign Truths. These are the "legacy" moments of the campaign.

Create costs 2 Power per rank (Core Rules §5). A 2-Momentum Truth starts at rank 1, and the same cost again raises it to rank 2 (Core Rules §8). A Scene Truth can’t stand in for a Site or Region change.

In a conflict, Discover, Advance and other off-menu Effects need a permitted action or explicit exception (Core Rules §10.6). Winning achieves the conflict’s stated intent, not every related project clock. A Truth can set up a scenery feature, but using it takes a scenery Maneuver (Core Rules §10.8). Preparation counts toward Superiority only under Core Rules §10.3.

C.7 Session Zero: Entanglements

Use this at the start of a campaign, after players have picked a Descriptor, Playbook and Focus but before Beliefs, Goals and tags are final. It takes about 60 to 90 minutes for four players. The result is a story map and your first Campaign Truths.

Elements. Each PC is a circle on the map. Everything else is placed on the map and joined to PCs by lines, each with a short description.

  • Character (square): a named NPC, such as a mentor, a rival, a debtor or a friend.

  • Organization (hexagon): a group that could become a faction (C.5, Module E).

  • Prop (diamond): an object that matters to the story.

  • Set (diamond): a place that matters.

  • Situation (triangle): an event or pressure, past, present or looming.

Phases. Go around the table once per phase. Each player adds elements and draws lines from their PC. A player can’t use the same shape twice in a row. Each phase also gives each PC something for their sheet:

Using the map.

  • Wedges. The GM picks one line to threaten in the first session.

  • Late arrivals. A new PC runs the same phases against the existing map.

Campaign Truths. Finish by writing 3 to 5 Campaign Truths (Core Rules §8) from the map. Each Situation is a candidate, and each Organization that most PCs touch gives one. Mark Organizations that conflict with each other as faction candidates for C.8.

Lines into factions. A line between a PC and an Organization suggests a Module E record. Keep the faction’s policy separate from personal ties:

  • Sworn member of, commissioned by: a membership or office Truth (E.4), with its permissions, duties and limits. A line alone doesn’t grant command.

  • Patron of, funded by: a patronage record (E.4): what’s supplied, what’s expected and when it ends.

  • Owes, is owed by: a favour (E.7) with agreed service, limits and terms, not an unlimited debt.

  • Feuds with, hunted by: adverse starting Standing, if it reflects the faction’s policy toward the party (E.3). Fears alone doesn’t change Standing.

  • Betrayed, estranged from: a personal exception for that PC (E.4). It replaces the normal relationship Truth instead of stacking with it.

Props and Sets tied to an Organization become faction assets (E.2) only if the faction actually owns, controls or can reach them. Interest alone isn’t possession.

Relationships aren’t free bonuses. Record relevant Truths normally. An ally or faction tie doesn’t by itself grant Help, Backing, an extra turn, its own Disposition or Superiority; apply those rules when the resource actually contributes (Core Rules §10.2–10.3, §10.9; Module E.6).

C.8 Building Factions: Long Knives

If Module E is in use, use E.1A instead. Otherwise, build the starting factions for C.5 like this:

  1. Place. Name the location. Record it as the first Region Truth (C.1).

  2. Gather. List the groups the PCs” lines point to: Organizations from the map, plus fragments of them. Note what each wants in the area and who would oppose it.

  3. Cluster. Pick about four factions that would find one another aggravating. Law or a local lord often belongs even if no PC touches them.

  4. Sheet. For each faction write:

  • Has: one obvious asset and one less obvious one. These set its rating: +1 minor, +2 solid, +3 major, +4 dominant.

  • Wants: its current project, with a clock sized by the change it makes (8 for a Site Truth, 12 for a Region Truth; C.6) and the result Truth it creates or changes when the clock fills.

  • Link: how it connects to the PCs.

  • Relations: allied or hostile toward each other faction. Where it matters, write the relation as a Truth (Core Rules §8).

  1. Incident. One faction does something underhanded or aggressive that affects the others. Treat it as a project that has already filled: record its result Truth. The PCs may see it but can’t prevent it.

  2. Reaction. Give each other faction a response as a linked clock (Core Rules §7), opened by the incident.

  3. Future. Write the result Truth each clock creates if the PCs do nothing. Make at least one visible to the players, and bad enough that they want to act.

  4. Involvement. Note how each PC is pulled in. Appeals from factions should be biased and based on limited information.

The incident is history. It happened before play starts; it can’t later cancel a PC’s action, skip a conflict or complete a project. Once play begins, use C.5 or Module E. Campaign Truths still need a major arc.

Module D — Downtime (Torchbearer, Imperium Maledictum)

Downtime is the stretch between expeditions when the party is safe enough to recover, prepare, build and answer to the people around them. It has three equal jobs: recover and prepare (treat injuries, restore tags, restock), make and grow (craft, research, adapt to loss) and connect (tend allies, settle obligations, push on the wider world). Module D adds no new currency. It runs on Checks, Power, clocks and Woe, all of which already exist.

D.1 When It Applies

  • Haven. Downtime begins when the party reaches a place with shelter, food and enough safety that nobody expects an attack tonight: a village, outpost, monastery, camp or home base. The GM decides.

  • Field rests stay separate. Core Rules §6.5 rests and Module B’s night’s rest and Grind recovery work as written, and give no Checks or endeavours. Reaching a haven isn’t a reset: pools, statuses and Scars follow their own recovery rules.

  • A stay ends when the party leaves the haven or is forced out. Finished work stands; clocks keep their progress.

  • Replaces Module B.4. If you use Module D, the Players” Turn follows these rules.

  • Stay Woe. The GM does not use expedition Woe in downtime. The stay starts with 1 Woe per PC. Woe gained from downtime rolls joins this pool, which pays for the stay’s event, complications and danger clocks, then is discarded (Core Rules §9.2).

  • World Turn (Module E). If Module E is in use, a stay contains one World Turn (E.10). It uses no Checks and no Stay Woe.

D.4 Endeavours

An endeavour is a stretch of focused effort during the stay.

  • Routine. If failure would add nothing and the outcome is certain, no roll is needed. It generates no Power and no Momentum (Core Rules §6.6).

  • Uncertain. If the result matters, roll the listed skill against Standard 15, adjusted by Truths. Spend Power on Effects (Core Rules §5). Unspent Power becomes Momentum, which decays.

  • Success at a Cost: the GM adds a consequence, or the stay pool gains 1 Woe (player’s choice, Core Rules §2.5). Failure: the effort doesn’t take, the stay pool gains 1 Woe, and the GM announces any consequence before another attempt.

  • Light or Strenuous. A character with a lasting injury who takes a Strenuous endeavour does not progress that injury’s healing interval this interval (Core Rules §6.6).

Scenes that become conflicts. A consequential, actively opposed struggle may need a scripted conflict instead of one endeavour roll (Core Rules §10.1). Then use the conflict rules (Core Rules §10.5–10.7), not the Standard 15 and Effect menu above. Checks and Help grant no extra scripted turn, and a shared endeavour doesn’t give each contributor their own Disposition.

Shared endeavours. A shared endeavour has one goal for the party or a subgroup. It costs 2 Checks, paid from the participants” pooled Checks.

  • Choose a lead. Each participant describes their contribution.

  • The lead rolls. Participants add Help as in Core Rules §2.4, within the +2 bonus-die cap.

  • Spend Power as usual. The result benefits all participants.

  • Participants need not pay equally. Non-participants pay nothing.

D.6a Reputation in Downtime

Downtime does not create reputation by itself. Distinguish the following:

  • Standing (E.3) changes only through consequential deeds a faction learns of and attributes to the party.

  • A reputation tag records what a character is known for. It comes from character growth or setting rules, not from a Connect endeavour, and counts toward the tag cap unless an ability says otherwise.

  • An ability may make a reputation a Campaign Truth, like the Bellator capstone Legend of the Field (Generic Playbooks §3.1).

  • A contact Truth (D.4 Connect) names a relationship with a person and what it makes possible. It isn’t a reputation tag and doesn’t change Standing.

Momentum, Woe and scene-scale Create can’t buy reputation (E.11). A relationship Truth (E.3) shifts Difficulty and a reputation tag adds to the roll; both can apply.

Module E — Factions, Standing & Obligations

Organizations have ambitions, resources, loyalties, and limits. This module connects their struggles to the party’s adventures: factions provide opportunities and assistance; the party’s actions determine what those factions can accomplish.

Use this module when alliances, patronage, collective action or political consequences matter. It is setting-neutral, and its numbers are playtest values.

When enabled, it replaces Module C.5’s faction actions, and E.1A replaces C.8; the rest of Module C still applies. Core rules apply to PC rolls, Effects, Truths, clocks and conflicts except where this module says otherwise.

E.1 Faction Records

A faction is an organized group pursuing a shared purpose: a household, village council, guild, temple, company of soldiers, criminal network, or administration.

Record:

  • Name, purpose, and long-term ambition.

  • Operational reach: the places and communities where it can act.

  • Five Qualities, each rated 0 to +4.

  • Two or three assets and one vulnerability.

  • Standing toward the party, from −3 to +3.

  • A representative who can explain or authorize requests.

  • One current project and its clock.

  • An important ally and rival, where relevant.

  • Outstanding favours, obligations, offices, and personal exceptions.

Prepare full records only for active factions. A name, purpose, and Standing suffice for a background group. Begin with three active factions; normally track no more than five at once.

The party may found or lead a faction. Give it ratings justified by its actual people and resources; do not purchase a second character sheet with personal pool points. Leadership grants only the authority its governing structure recognizes.

E.1A Building the Starting Cluster (Long Knives)

Use this when starting a campaign, after the C.7 story map is drawn. It produces the first faction records (E.1), their starting Standing and the opening situation, and takes about 30 to 45 minutes. It replaces C.8 when Module E is enabled.

Size. Aim for four factions: three active (full records) and one background (name, purpose and Standing).

  1. Place. Name the location. Record it as the first Region Truth (C.1).

  2. Gather. List the groups the PCs” lines point to: Organizations from the map, plus fragments of them. Note what each wants in the area and who would oppose it.

  3. Cluster. Pick four groups that would find one another aggravating. Law, a local lord or an imperial office often belong even if no PC touches them.

  4. Record. Fill in each active faction’s record (E.1) using the table.

Qualities measure capability, not fixed population or geographical size. A small veteran force can have strong Might. A mobile caravan can have Territory through its vehicles, workforce, and supply arrangements.

Explain every nonzero rating through concrete assets. Influence +3 might mean couriers, shipping agents, and sympathetic officials. It does not mean the faction knows every secret.

A Quality of 0 does not prohibit its members from acting as individuals. It means the organization cannot independently mount a major action in that field without establishing the missing capability or securing outside support.

A permanent Cohesion of 0 is a crisis when a faction has fallen to it (E.12). A faction built at Cohesion 0 begins with its Fracture clock created and no automatic tick that turn.

Assets and reach

Assets determine what the faction can actually do. A treasury cannot occupy a town without personnel; soldiers cannot cross a sea without transport. A rating never creates an unstated army, fleet, office, or network.

When an asset matters mechanically, record it as a Truth with scope, rank and removal condition. It adds its concrete advantage, not a second bonus restating the Quality.

Record a distant branch separately when local capabilities matter. The imperial institution’s full resources are not automatically available to every provincial official.

Changing Qualities

Change a permanent rating only after a lasting change to the capacity it represents. Losing one shipment normally changes an asset, creates Strain, or sets back a project. Losing the principal treasury and credit network may reduce Treasure.

An improvement normally requires an appropriate 8- or 12-segment project, a concrete new resource, and an outcome large enough to justify the next rating. Repetitive minor jobs cannot manufacture permanent growth. No Quality exceeds +4.

Changing Standing

Change Standing for consequential deeds the faction learns about and attributes to the party:

  • +1: complete important work, protect a valued asset, uphold a difficult agreement, or make a meaningful sacrifice.

  • +2: avert an organizational catastrophe or deliver a transformative victory.

  • −1: cause significant avoidable loss, break a meaningful agreement, or seriously undermine the faction.

  • −2: betray it, destroy an irreplaceable asset, or deliberately deliver it into an enemy’s hands.

Apply changes once per undertaking, normally at its conclusion or when the consequences become known. Usually apply a net change of at most one step per faction per adventure; reserve two steps for decisive events. Clamp Standing between −3 and +3.

Paid work may demonstrate reliability. Honest failure is not betrayal. Declining an ordinary job is not an automatic penalty. Trivial gifts and repeated errands do not improve Standing indefinitely.

Secret deeds create no immediate public change. Record pending exposure when necessary. False accusations may change treatment; record the accusation as a Truth so disproving it offers a route to repair.

Helping one faction does not automatically offend every rival. Change another faction’s Standing only when its interests were meaningfully affected and it knows whom to credit or blame.

E.4 Personal Exceptions and Authority

Use party Standing by default. Record an individual exception only when a particular relationship matters: an estranged child of the leader, a sworn member, an acknowledged oathbreaker, or someone who saved a representative’s life.

Give the character an effective Standing for that relationship when needed. Party +1 and a character’s temple exception −2 means the temple normally welcomes the group but treats that character as hostile in relevant dealings.

An exception applies to its subject, not automatically to everyone standing beside them. A trusted member can arrange an audience for companions without making them trusted members. Personal deeds change the exception unless the faction reasonably holds the whole party responsible.

Membership and office

Record membership, commissions, and offices as scoped Truths, not another numerical Influence track. Examples include Sworn Member, Commissioned Envoy, Licensed Supplier, and Captain of the Northern Watch.

State the permissions, jurisdiction, duration, duties, and removal conditions. Authority requires appointment, election, sponsorship, initiation, or another established process. Standing may open that opportunity; it does not replace it.

A legitimate seal may make access possible. It does not substitute Standing or a faction Quality for Persuade or another PC Skill.

Patronage

Record what a patron supplies, the service expected, what lies outside the arrangement, and what ends it. Routine agreed service is not a new favour each time. Refusal matters according to the actual agreement, not an unlimited claim on the character.

E.5 Seeking Aid

  1. State the assistance, timing, location, authorizing representative, burden, and offered compensation.

  2. Check capability, reach, available assets, and the representative’s authority.

  3. Grant willing, available, inconsequential assistance without a roll.

  4. Use a single core check for limited uncertainty; use an Argument or Negotiation conflict when the core conflict criteria are met.

  5. If the request is impossible now, explain what would have to change.

E.6 Faction Support in Party Scenes

A Quality establishes what support the faction can organize; Standing, authority, and obligations govern whether it is provided. No faction Quality is added directly to a PC roll.

Support has one concrete application:

  • Permission: equipment, credentials, transport, or access makes the action possible, or removes the need for a particular roll.

  • A scoped Truth: actual conditions change Difficulty under Core Rules §8.

  • Active Help: a participating ally uses the core Help procedure, pays its normal cost from its own pools and shares the risk. An ordinary NPC has no pools, so it can’t Help unless a rule says otherwise (Core Rules §6.1). Qualities aren’t pools.

Do not award all three for the same contribution. Stronger Qualities increase feasible reach, reliability, and scale, not an automatic numerical bonus. Might +4 can organize support beyond Might +1; it does not grant +4 Fight.

Accompanying allies. A companion is either a participant (joins rotation, uses its own ratings, bound by the stakes) or an asset (gives its stated benefit, takes no turn), never both. It doesn’t get its own Disposition, and removing it costs none, though a side loses when nobody can pursue its objective (Core Rules §10.9).

Backing gives +1 Disposition per eligible backer and costs no pool points (Core Rules §10.2). A faction’s size or personnel doesn’t by itself supply backers.

An escort may make a friendly checkpoint automatic. It cannot make every later negotiation easier simply because it exists. Personnel face the real risks of deployment, and a borrowed resource is not freely duplicable or permanently transferred.

E.7 Favours and Obligations

A favour is a specific acknowledged promise. An obligation is the same record from the debtor’s side. Record debtor, creditor, service, limits, notice, deadline or duration, and what settles it. Record who can call it in and whether it benefits the party or a named individual.

Agree to scope when the promise is made. “You owe us something” is an unresolved negotiation, not a blank cheque. Favours can be payment for work, part of an alliance, or compensation for aid.

Calling in a feasible, acknowledged favour within its terms requires no request check. Roll only for a separate uncertainty: evading inspection, locating a vessel, or reaching a destination. Broader or newly dangerous work may require renegotiation; establish whether the changed circumstances fall within the original agreement before rolling.

Possible settlements include fulfilment, an accepted substitute, partial settlement, or an agreed delay. Genuine inability is not automatically betrayal, though concealment or indifference may damage trust.

Knowingly refusing a feasible agreed obligation normally costs one Standing; severe betrayal may cost two. Owing a favour does not remove a player’s control over their character. State the consequences and let them choose.

Calling in an earned favour does not reduce Standing. Repaying one does not automatically improve it. Falling Standing does not erase outstanding promises. A favour transfers only with the debtor’s consent.

A favour grants its stated service, not automatic success, free Help, an extra scripted turn or control over another participant. A promise given as part of a concession or another benefit’s payment is a protected payment (Core Rules §10.7); renegotiating it needs agreement.

E.8 Adventures and Strategic Advantages

Before an undertaking, explain its likely strategic effect when the characters can reasonably foresee it. “Secure the bridge and our forces can cross this turn with a rank-1 advantage” gives the players a meaningful choice.

Successful adventures may:

  • Accomplish an objective outright.

  • Advance or obstruct a project through the Advance effect.

  • Establish a scoped Truth for a later faction action.

Examples: Enemy Deployment Exposed; Crossing Secured; Troops Paid Before the Muster; Reformers Have Public Support; Southern Squadron Will Not Intervene.

Record scope, rank, duration, and removal condition. Rank 1 is normal; rank 2 requires a decisive advantage. One-use preparation applies to the next relevant action and then expires; persistent advantages endure only as long as their established circumstances do. Apply the core Truth cap.

A roll does not automatically create a lasting Truth merely because it succeeds: use the appropriate core Effect or establish it as the agreed outcome of the completed undertaking. Larger lasting changes need the appropriate project or milestone, as in Module C.6.

Do not require a faction roll to confirm what the party already accomplished. Capturing a bridge does not lead to another roll to see whether it was captured. A separate roll may determine whether the army exploits that opportunity.

Distinct consequences can follow one achievement, but never award duplicate progress or duplicate modifiers for the same work. One bad PC roll does not automatically damage a whole faction; consequences must reach its people or resources.

One contribution. If a PC endeavour does the work a faction’s project action would do (the party sabotages the project itself), that endeavour replaces the faction’s own roll for that project this turn. Never count both for the same work.

A played conflict still uses its permitted actions: Advance or Discover needs an appropriate action or explicit exception. Attack or Feint Disposition loss isn’t project progress, and victory grants the stated objective, not unrelated clocks (Core Rules §10.6, §10.12).

E.9 Commitments and Strain

A faction normally takes one major project action per World Turn without automatic Strain. Exceptional deployment can exhaust a capability.

Mark one relevant Quality as Strained when the faction takes an additional major action, makes an exceptional deployment to support the party, overextends to meet a deadline, or suffers an explicit exhaustion consequence. Ordinary hospitality, meals, and introductions do not qualify.

A Strained Quality operates one rating lower, minimum 0. Strain doesn’t stack and isn’t also an adverse Truth for the same exhaustion. A faction can take at most one additional major action per turn. It must Strain that action’s primary Quality, which can’t already be Strained, so additional actions can’t repeat indefinitely.

Timing. Mark Strain when the action is declared (E.10, step 2), so that action and every other action that phase use the lowered rating.

Clear Strain after one full World Turn in which the affected capability is not used for a major action or exceptional commitment, provided recovery remains plausible. Ordinary background activity does not prevent recovery. Strain alone reducing effective Cohesion to 0 does not trigger Fracture; use permanent Cohesion for E.12.

Exhaustion is not destruction. Rest can recover tired guards; it cannot rebuild a burned warehouse. Track persistent losses separately.

E.10 World Turns

Choose one cadence: each in-world week, between adventures, or another agreed meaningful interval. Do not take extra turns simply because a session ended. This procedure replaces Module C.5 while Module E is enabled.

With Module D. The cadence is between adventures: one World Turn per stay. Strain (E.9), Fracture clocks (E.12) and project clocks count in World Turns. A long stay doesn’t recover Strain faster, and a short one doesn’t skip it.

  1. Resolve known consequences of recent play; update Standing, obligations, assets, and exceptions.

  2. Declare each active faction’s project action, primary Quality, intended outcome, and any exceptional commitment.

  3. Resolve actions and opposition. Use ratings at the start of the action phase; apply resulting permanent changes after all declared actions resolve. This prevents turn order from erasing a declared action without warning.

  4. Resolve completed clocks, crises, and eligible Strain recovery.

  5. Reveal observable developments through news, witnesses, altered conditions, or encounters. Secret projects need not reveal their details, but consequential changes should leave discoverable signs.

Organizational resolution

Roll 2d12 + the relevant effective Quality against Standard 15, adjusted by task difficulty and relevant Truths. Use one primary Quality, not the sum of two. Other capabilities may be prerequisites or provide distinct concrete circumstances; their ratings are not automatic extra modifiers.

  • Success: 1 Power, plus 1 per full 3 points over the target.

  • Success at a Cost (miss by 1–3): 1 Power and a concrete setback appropriate to the action.

  • Failure (miss by 4+): no progress; introduce a relevant setback.

  • Doubles follow the core’s success/cost/failure twist rules, including double 12s and double 1s.

Organizational exception: off-screen faction actions do not gain or spend the party’s Momentum, PC pools, or Tychē Points, and do not generate or spend Woe. Uncommitted Power is discarded. Resolve twists narratively; there is no off-screen Woe buy-off. PC scenes retain all normal core currencies.

Commit Power at one segment per Power. A single faction action advances one declared project or produces one limited immediate result; it cannot purchase unrelated outcomes. Cap progress at the remaining segments; discard overflow. Link clocks for wider ambitions.

Opposition and disruption

A faction may spend its action obstructing a known project if it has a credible method. Roll its relevant Quality against Standard 15, adjusted by the target’s concrete defences and other Truths. The target’s Quality rating is not automatically a Difficulty shift.

On success, spend Power to remove uncompleted progress, one segment per Power. Remove no more than is present and discard excess. A defending faction may devote its own action to countermeasures, producing appropriate protection or removing the obstructing capability. Do not add a free reaction roll for every attempted disruption.

Declare obstruction or countermeasures in step 2 as the faction’s one major action. A faction that defends does not also advance its own project.

Apply all same-turn progress and disruption to a clock together before checking completion. A clock that completes cannot later be “unticked”: reversing its established result needs a new undertaking.

If two clocks with contradictory result Truths complete in the same turn, neither result is established as written: the GM and players agree on a compromise Truth, and both factions are Strained.

PCs can use Advance to remove progress when sabotage or delay directly supports that effect. Establish the affected project before the roll. A played scene replaces the equivalent off-screen work, not the whole faction’s unrelated activity.

Completion

A project grants its declared result, not every benefit the victor might want. Create, remove, or change a Truth when appropriate; record scope, rank, and removal condition. Completion does not automatically make a Truth rank 2 or increase a Quality.

E.11 Conflict, Objectives, and Pressure

Use core Argument, Negotiation, or Battle conflicts when the action deserves a played conflict. Use normal core Disposition, scripting, Power, concessions, and compromise.

Each side has one shared Disposition total, not a track for every faction member. Defeat doesn’t by itself cause injury, permanent organizational loss or every objective the winner wants. Use Core Rules §10.12 for mutual defeat and Core Rules §10.13 for compromise. E.10’s compromise Truth for contradictory project clocks is a separate procedure.

Standing grants no automatic Superiority. Insider intelligence, an arranged audience, or a secured guarantee can qualify as actual preparation under the core’s categories and cap. Evaluate distinct advantages, not labels. Combat receives no direct Standing modifier.

State strategic objectives separately:

  • Raid: take or destroy specified resources.

  • Occupy: hold a particular place.

  • Break resistance: disable a specified fighting force.

  • Humiliate: undermine prestige before a relevant audience.

  • Annex: establish lasting control over a holding.

  • Reconcile: restore cooperation between internal groups.

Winning a battle does not automatically create stable government. Occupation may open a separate integration project and establish Population Rejects the Occupation. One conflict does not grant unrelated spoils or objectives beyond its stakes and compromise.

Woe in faction scenes

Use Woe normally during PC scenes to introduce supported complications, reinforcements, or danger-clock progress. Hostility is a motive, not an unlimited Woe source. A completed World Turn project establishes its result without an extra Woe payment; a new scene complication uses the normal rules. Never charge twice for the same event.

Inside scripted conflicts, failure and Success at a Cost follow Core Rules §10.7: no unrelated GM move, automatic injury or extra Disposition loss, or ordinary-failure Woe for losing a versus test. Specials, hazards, Risky rolls and other explicit consequences keep their stated effects and timing. Woe can’t change a revealed script or cancel actions that resolve on their own.

Woe cannot directly buy a Standing reduction. It can expose evidence or create circumstances that cause one. Momentum and scene-scale Create effects likewise cannot buy permanent reputation.

E.12 Damage, Crisis, and Reorganization

Target the capability actually harmed. Destroying payroll may damage Treasure; exposing agents may damage Influence; losing an estate may damage Territory; defeat may damage Might; betrayal may provoke a Cohesion crisis.

Use an asset loss, setback, adverse Truth or Strain for limited harm. Permanent rating loss needs a lasting reduction in capacity, set by the stakes or a completed undertaking, not a lost roll. Ordinary Disposition loss doesn’t reduce a Quality, mark Strain, remove an asset or tick Fracture.

Fracture

When permanent Cohesion reaches 0, establish an internal crisis and start a 4-segment Fracture clock. Advance it once at the end of each subsequent World Turn that ends with permanent Cohesion still 0. A newly created clock does not receive this automatic tick in its creation turn. Explicit consequences may advance it further.

Restoring permanent Cohesion stops automatic progress but does not erase existing fractures. Reconciliation or another concrete undertaking removes progress. When the clock fills, the faction splits, disbands, loses central authority, or transforms according to the established situation. Its people and assets do not simply vanish.

Branches, alliances, and mergers

Separate a local branch when its resources or loyalties differ materially. Do not use a resource twice as if parent and branch had independent copies. An alliance provides agreed access and obligations, not summed ratings.

A merger is a reorganization project. Reassess Qualities from the actual combined capacity once integration occurs; do not automatically add ratings or increase matched scores. Hostile absorption may bring assets while reducing Cohesion or creating adverse Truths. Defeating a smaller organization can grant a useful asset without increasing a rating.

E.13 Example: Reed Quay Association

An illustrative faction, not setting canon.

Field

Record

Purpose

Protect river trade and control profitable landing places.

Reach

Lower river settlements and commercial offices in the provincial capital.

Might +1

Dock guards and armed crews.

Treasure +3

Freight income and commercial credit.

Influence +2

Agents, weighmasters, and clerks.

Territory +2

Barges, warehouses, and landing rights.

Members cooperate primarily for profit.

Standing +2

Trusted; Nera, master of the eastern landing, represents the association.

Vulnerability

Its principal charter is legally disputed.

Project

Secure Exclusive Ferry Rights, 8 segments: a lasting local institutional change.

Favour

Conceal up to six people aboard one outbound barge this season, with a day’s notice; no declared contraband.

The party requests public freight records. Nera willingly supplies them; no roll is needed. Access to sealed judicial records is a different request, possibly requiring an introduction or commission.

The association funds a lawful journey and provides a commercial introduction. Passage permits the journey; the introduction grants an audience. Neither grants +3 Persuade because Treasure is +3.

The party retrieves admissible evidence that a rival obtained a charter through bribery. As the agreed outcome of the undertaking, Rival Charter Under Legal Challenge becomes a rank-1 Truth for the ferry-rights project until the challenge is adjudicated. If the party obtains a final judgment itself, do not roll again to establish that judgment.

On the World Turn, the association uses Influence +2. The evidence lowers the Standard 15 target to Easy 12. Dice of 7 and 9 total 16; +2 gives 18, producing 3 Power. It commits three segments. Without the evidence, the same total against 15 would produce 2 Power.

Later, the party saves the winter grain convoy at substantial personal risk. Once the association learns what happened, Standing may rise to +3. A new favour is not automatic: the association must explicitly promise one.

If the association undertakes an additional urgent funding action that turn, Treasure becomes Strained before that action and operates at +2. It cannot take a third major action. Recovery needs a later full turn without a major Treasure commitment and with plausible access to its credit network.

Module F: Inquiry & Revelation

Use this module when a mystery is the point of the game: a murder, a vanished caravan, a forbidden text, a buried ruin. It separates finding from understanding. Finding the clue is never the challenge. Reading it correctly is.

The module changes nothing outside Inquiry scenes. Conflicts, travel, Resistance, Momentum and Woe all work as written.

F.1 The Core Rule

If a scene holds a core clue, and a character has a fitting skill rated +1 or higher, that character finds it. No roll. No cost. No failure.

  • Core clue. A fact the group needs to reach the next scene or to understand the Truth of the case. Every Inquiry scene has at least one.

  • Fitting skill. Any skill from the setting’s skill list that the GM can reasonably tie to the clue. Several skills can reach the same clue by different routes.

  • Description. The player says what the character does and which skill it uses, or the GM offers the clue unprompted to a character who clearly has it.

  • Stuck groups. If nobody has a fitting skill, the clue floats (F.6) to another source. A core clue is never locked behind a missing skill.

A found clue grants no Power, extra scripted turn or script change, and doesn’t count as success at a conflict action. Never require a Probe or Buy to get the guaranteed core clue.

F.2 Insight: The Extra Layer

A core clue gets you to the next scene. Insight is everything beyond that: motive, timing, who else was involved, what the culprit will do next, a shortcut, an ally. Insight is optional, and you can gain it in two ways.

Method

How it works

Probe

Roll 2d12 + the skill vs. a Difficulty set by the GM (usually Standard 15). Each Power you spend on Discover (Core Rules §5) is one Insight. The usual outcomes apply: a miss by 1–3 is a Success at a Cost, and a miss by 4+ is a Failure that gives the GM 1 Woe. A failed Probe never removes a core clue.

Buy

Spend 4 − Edge points (minimum 1) from the skill’s pool, with no roll. You gain exactly one Insight. The pool is the one that pays for that skill in Effort and Resistance (Core Rules §3.3).

Probe is cheap but risky. Buy is safe but drains the pool you also need for Resistance and Effort. Players should have to choose between them.

Payment. Apply discounts and the minimum, then pay the Buy in full. It’s a voluntary purchase, not Resistance, so you can’t overrun an insufficient pool (Core Rules §6.1); spending the last points still runs the pool dry.

Which pool pays?
Sōma: long vigils, exhumations, hard physical labour. Mētis: meticulous search, tailing, lock and mechanism work. Psychē: deduction, records, anatomy, languages. Pneuma: reading people, rites, dread places, the uncanny. The GM may allow a different pool when the fiction supports it (Core Rules §3.3).

F.3 What an Insight Can Be

When you gain an Insight, choose one. The GM may veto a choice that does not fit the scene.

  • Detail. A true fact that deepens a clue you already have. It can help you solve the case, but it is never required to reach the next scene.

  • Leverage. Create a Truth that fits what you learned (the guard captain is afraid of the cellar). It lasts for the scene, or until the case ends if you pay 1 more: 1 Power from a Probe, or 1 more pool point for a Buy.

  • Contact. Name an ally, informant or source, and say how you know them. The GM adds a short tag.

  • Spotlight. Show real expertise in front of others. You gain a free invoke of one tag or Truth about your standing, once per scene. Use this to shine, not to win a fight.

  • Foresight. The GM tells you one thing the opposition is about to do.

If no Insight fits, nothing happens and no points are lost. Buy costs are refunded.

Benefit scope. A Leverage Truth uses the normal rank, scope and removal condition (Core Rules §8); its case-long extension is an Inquiry exception, not a change to Create.

A Contact provides the stated relationship, not Standing, a reputation tag, free Help or a turn. A Spotlight invoke awards no Standing or reputation. Foresight is information, not a script change or a way to cancel the opposition’s action (Core Rules §10.5).

F.4 Boosted Skills and Recovery

  • Boosted skill. At creation, mark one skill you hold at +1 or higher. When you Buy an Insight with it, regain 1 point to the paying pool. Mark another skill each time you have taken 4 Advances.

  • Recovery. Pools recover as usual (Core Rules §6). The GM may also offer a refresh opportunity: a night in an archive, a round of drinks in the right tavern, a meal with a scholar. Each restores 2 points to the pool that fits.

  • Help. One ally may Help a Probe as normal (Core Rules §2.4), or help a Buy by lowering its cost by 1 (minimum 1).

  • Tags. A power tag that applies to the skill adds +1 to a Probe, as usual. Burning one gives +1 Power.

Pay before boosted-skill recovery: the regained point isn’t a discount, and it doesn’t clear a running-dry status the payment caused. A refresh opportunity is offered by the GM, not an automatic refill at the start or end of an Inquiry scene.

Probe Help and tags keep their normal caps (+2 power tags, +2 bonus dice). The Buy discount isn’t a bonus die or an extra Insight.

F.5 Danger and the Clock

Inquiry has no failure state, so it needs pressure from somewhere else. Pressure comes from the opposition, not from missed rolls.

  • Woe pays for Reaction. When a Probe fails, or when the group lingers, the GM gains Woe as normal. The GM may spend Woe on an Antagonist Reaction: the culprit destroys evidence, a witness vanishes, a patron grows impatient, an ambush is arranged.

  • The case clock. Start a 6-segment clock (Core Rules §7) called They Know We Know. Tick it when the group makes noise, takes a long time, or when a Probe fails by 4+. When it fills, the opposition acts decisively.

  • Danger is a separate scene. A scene either asks “what do we learn?” (Inquiry) or “what do we survive?” (Conflict, Hazard). A scene that is both is two scenes: finish one, then start the other.

  • Clues never depend on winning a fight. Winning may give Insight, but a core clue must be reachable even if the group loses, flees or is captured.

Only a failed Probe ticks the case clock, not ordinary failed conflict actions. Antagonist Reactions respect established facts: destroying a source can’t make required information unreachable.

Splitting a scene doesn’t make a continuing struggle a new conflict. Disposition, injury trades, Taken Out participants and ongoing effects carry forward, and pools don’t refresh (Core Rules §10.2).

F.6 Building a Case

Work backward from the Truth, then forward through scenes.

  1. The Truth. Write one paragraph: who, what, why, and how it was done.

  2. The Hook. Tie it to a Belief, Goal or Instinct of at least one PC.

  3. The Twist. Something that widens or changes the case halfway through.

  4. The Conclusion. A confrontation, a choice, or a public accusation. Core clues bring the group here, but the players decide what to do with what they know.

  5. The scenes. Link them with clues, as below.

Clue type

Use

Core clue

Points to a scene or to the Truth. Always found, never rolled for.

Lead-in

A clue that points to a scene. Give every scene at least two lead-ins from different scenes, so the case is a web and not a line.

Floating clue

The GM places it wherever the group looks first. Use it for the clue the case cannot do without.

Leveraged clue

Available only once the group holds another clue. Example: a suspect clams up until you confront them with the ledger.

Pipe clue

A detail that seems like colour now and pays off later.

Red herring

True, interesting and misleading. Never a lie told by the narrator. Give each one a source and a reason.

Scene types: Introduction, Core (holds core clues), Alternate (another route to a clue), Subplot, Action (Conflict or Hazard), Reaction (the opposition responds) and Conclusion.

F.7 Running Inquiry

  • Cover the skills. Build an Investigator Matrix, a table of the party’s skills against the case’s scenes. Give each PC a scene where their skills matter most.

  • Be generous. Give information freely. Let players chase red herrings. Give minor NPCs a past and a want.

  • Core clues lead to the end, not through it. Following them only gets the party to the last scene. Insight and good reasoning decide whether they arrive prepared.

  • Listen and improvise. When players theorise, borrow their best ideas, provided they do not contradict a core clue or the Truth.

  • Share the world. A good Insight lets the player describe the contact, the rumour or the detail, within the Truths already established.

  • Prompt early. For the first session or two, remind players what their skills can do.

F.8 Weird Methods

Oracles, divinations, scrying, speaking to the dead and similar methods are tools that produce clues, not answers. An Arcana-style skill finds core clues like any other. The result is a true but partial, symbolic or conditional reading. Use Pneuma for the Buy or Probe. Opponents who expect scrying can block it, mislead it, or leave a false trail with their own working. Let the setting decide whether such evidence counts as proof.

Quick Reference
Core clue: fitting skill (+1 or higher) → found, free.
Probe: 2d12 + skill vs. Standard 15; each Power on Discover = 1 Insight.
Buy: 4 − Edge (minimum 1) from the skill’s pool = 1 Insight, no roll; pay in full.
Boosted skill: Buy with it → regain 1 point after payment.
Case clock: 6 segments; tick on noise, delay, or a Probe failed by 4+.
Danger: a separate scene, not an automatic resource reset; clues never depend on winning.
